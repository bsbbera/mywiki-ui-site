# Leontief Paradox

<p class="at-lead">
The Leontief Paradox is the empirical finding that US exports were less capital-intensive than its imports — contradicting the Heckscher-Ohlin theorem, which predicts that capital-abundant countries export capital-intensive goods.
</p>

## Overview

Wassily Leontief's 1953 input-output analysis of US trade data revealed the paradox, shaking confidence in simple factor-proportions trade theory. Explanations include: human capital is omitted (US exports are skill-intensive), technology differences exist, demand patterns differ, trade barriers distort flows, and the data period was atypical.

The paradox stimulated richer trade theories: intra-industry trade (Krugman), product differentiation, economies of scale, and technology-gap models. It remains a classic example of how empirical anomalies drive theoretical refinement in economics.
