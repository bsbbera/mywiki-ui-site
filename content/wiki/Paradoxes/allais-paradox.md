# Allais Paradox

<p class="at-lead">
In 1953, Maurice Allais demonstrated that people's choices systematically violate the independence axiom of expected utility theory. A change in a shared possible outcome across alternatives affects preferences in ways that standard theory cannot explain. The Allais paradox became a cornerstone of behavioural economics and directly influenced prospect theory.
</p>

## Overview

The Allais paradox reveals that decision-makers care not only about final outcomes and their probabilities, but also about the pattern of possible outcomes. The certainty effect — overweighting outcomes deemed certain relative to merely probable ones — explains the classic preference reversals. Kahneman and Tversky later incorporated this into prospect theory as a core empirical finding.
