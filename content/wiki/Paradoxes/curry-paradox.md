# Curry's Paradox

<p class="at-lead">
"If this sentence is true, then Germany borders China." If the sentence is true, its antecedent is true, so Germany borders China. If false, the conditional is vacuously true, so the sentence is true after all. A single self-referential conditional seems to entail any arbitrary conclusion.
</p>

## Overview

Curry's paradox shows that a language containing its own truth predicate and allowing unrestricted self-reference is inconsistent — not because of negation, but because of implication. The standard resolution restricts self-referential truth predicates through Tarski's hierarchy of languages or similar stratification devices.
