# Grossman-Stiglitz Paradox

<p class="at-lead">
The Grossman-Stiglitz Paradox demonstrates that perfectly efficient markets are logically impossible. If prices already reflect all available information, then no one would incur the cost of acquiring information — yet without informed traders, prices would not reflect information.
</p>

## Overview

Published in 1980, the paradox shows that market efficiency is a matter of degree, not absolutes. There must be **noise traders** (uninformed participants) and **information rents** (profits to informed traders) to sustain the incentive for information gathering. The equilibrium is one where markets are efficient enough that marginal profits from information equal marginal costs.

The paradox undermines strong-form market efficiency and rationalises the existence of active management, research departments, and information markets. Joseph Stiglitz won the 2001 Nobel Prize in Economics for his work on asymmetric information, including this paradox.
