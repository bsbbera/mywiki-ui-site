## Interesting facts

- The word "paradox" comes from the Greek *para doxa*: "beyond belief."
- Several paradoxes appear in multiple domains — the St. Petersburg paradox sits at the intersection of probability, decision theory, and economics.
- Zeno's paradoxes, formulated around 450 BCE, remain active topics in mathematical analysis and philosophy today.

## Interview questions

- Why does the Monty Hall problem feel so counterintuitive, and what does that reveal about human probabilistic reasoning?
- How does Simpson's paradox challenge the way we interpret aggregated data in machine learning and policy?
- Is the Ship of Theseus a problem about identity, or a problem about language?
