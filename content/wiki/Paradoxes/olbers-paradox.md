> *If the universe is infinite and static, the night sky should be as bright as the surface of a star.*
> — Heinrich Wilhelm Olbers

# Olbers' Paradox

<p class="at-lead">
Olbers' Paradox asks why the night sky is dark if the universe is infinite, eternal, and uniformly populated with stars. Every line of sight should eventually terminate on a star, making the entire sky uniformly bright — yet it is not.
</p>

## Overview

The paradox was articulated by Heinrich Olbers in 1823, though earlier thinkers (Kepler, Halley, Cheseaux) noted the problem. The resolution requires abandoning one or more assumptions of the static infinite universe. Modern cosmology resolves the paradox through the **Big Bang** (finite age means light from distant stars hasn't reached us), **cosmic expansion** (redshift dims distant light), and **finite stellar lifetimes**.

Olbers' paradox is historically significant because it provided early evidence against the steady-state infinite universe and pointed toward a dynamic, evolving cosmos. It illustrates how a simple observation — the darkness of night — can constrain fundamental cosmological models.
