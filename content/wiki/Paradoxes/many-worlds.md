# Many-Worlds Interpretation

<p class="at-lead">
The Many-Worlds Interpretation (MWI) of quantum mechanics, proposed by Hugh Everett in 1957, asserts that every quantum measurement causes the universe to branch into parallel worlds, each containing one possible outcome. There is no wave-function collapse — only relentless splitting.
</p>

## Overview

MWI resolves the measurement problem by treating the observer as part of the quantum system. When a particle is measured in superposition, the observer also enters superposition, with each branch experiencing a definite outcome. From within any branch, it appears as if collapse occurred — but globally, all outcomes persist.

The interpretation eliminates the need for a collapse postulate or special role for consciousness, but at the cost of an enormous ontology of parallel universes. Debates centre on whether the branches are "real," how probabilities emerge in a deterministic theory, and whether MWI is empirically distinguishable from other interpretations.
