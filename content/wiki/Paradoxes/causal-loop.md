# Causal Loop

<p class="at-lead">
A Causal Loop, also called a bootstrap or ontological paradox, is a time-travel paradox in which an event is caused by itself. An object or information travels back in time and becomes the very thing that later leads to its own origin — with no independent point of creation.
</p>

## Overview

Classic examples include taking Shakespeare's complete works back in time and giving them to young Shakespeare, or a time traveller becoming their own parent. The paradox challenges our intuitive notion that every effect has a preceding cause. In physics, it raises questions about the consistency of closed timelike curves in general relativity.

The **Novikov Self-Consistency Principle** resolves the paradox by asserting that only self-consistent histories are physically possible — the loop must be stable. Alternatively, some interpretations invoke multiple timelines or quantum mechanics to avoid logical contradiction.
