# Goodman's Paradox

<p class="at-lead">
Goodman's Paradox, also known as the New Riddle of Induction, challenges the assumption that observing regular patterns provides rational grounds for prediction. Goodman introduces the predicate "grue" (green before time t, blue after) to show that not all regularities are law-like.
</p>

## Overview

In *Fact, Fiction, and Forecast* (1955), Nelson Goodman argues that purely syntactic definitions of law-like statements fail. Every emerald observed before time t is both green and grue. If induction supports "all emeralds are green," it equally supports "all emeralds are grue" — but these predict opposite colours after t.

The paradox forces a distinction between "projectible" predicates (green) and "non-projectible" ones (grue). Goodman's solution invokes "entrenchment" — predicates that have been successfully used in past inductions are projectible. The paradox reshaped philosophy of science, influencing debates on simplicity, natural kinds, and Bayesian confirmation theory.
