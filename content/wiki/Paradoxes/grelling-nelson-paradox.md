# Grelling–Nelson Paradox

<p class="at-lead">
An adjective is "autological" if it applies to itself (e.g., "English" is English) and "heterological" if it does not (e.g., "long" is not long). Is "heterological" heterological? Either answer produces a contradiction — a semantic analogue of Russell's paradox applied to adjectives.
</p>

## Overview

The Grelling–Nelson paradox reveals that self-applicability is treacherous even in natural language. Like Russell's paradox, it dissolves once we stratify predicates into levels: the classification "heterological" belongs to a meta-level above the adjectives it describes, preventing direct self-application.
