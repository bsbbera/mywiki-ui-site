> [!quote]
> *The more the community as a whole saves, the lower its aggregate income may become.*
> — John Maynard Keynes

# Paradox of Thrift

<p class="at-lead">
The Paradox of Thrift states that when everyone increases saving simultaneously, total savings may not rise — and can even fall — because reduced consumption lowers aggregate demand, income, and output. What is rational for an individual becomes harmful for the economy.
</p>

## Overview

In a recession, individuals sensibly save more as a precaution. But if everyone does so, spending drops, businesses earn less, workers are laid off, and incomes fall — reducing the total pool available to save from. This is a central Keynesian argument for stimulus: government spending can offset the decline in private consumption.

The paradox assumes a demand-constrained economy with unused capacity. In full-employment economies, increased saving finances investment and raises long-run growth (the classical view). The debate between Keynesian and classical perspectives remains central to macroeconomic policy.
