# Fitch's Paradox of Knowability

<p class="at-lead">
Fitch's Paradox of Knowability demonstrates that if every truth is potentially knowable, then every truth is actually known — a conclusion most philosophers reject. It poses a deep challenge to anti-realist philosophies that identify truth with verifiability or knowability.
</p>

## Overview

Published by Frederic Fitch in 1963, the paradox uses modal logic to show that the claim "all truths are knowable" ($\forall p (p \rightarrow \Diamond Kp)$) entails "all truths are known" ($\forall p (p \rightarrow Kp)$). The proof constructs a proposition $q$ that is true but unknown; if $q$ were knowable, someone could know it, contradicting its definition.

Responses include restricting the knowability thesis to basic truths, revising modal logic, or abandoning anti-realism. The paradox remains influential in epistemology, philosophy of mind, and the semantics of intuitionistic logic.
