# Paradox of Tolerance

<p class="at-lead">
The Paradox of Tolerance, formulated by Karl Popper in 1945, argues that a society that is completely tolerant — without limits — is vulnerable to destruction by the intolerant. Tolerance, taken to its extreme, may need to be withdrawn from those who would abolish it.
</p>

## Overview

Popper's formulation in *The Open Society and Its Enemies* does not advocate suppressing all intolerant views, but warns that tolerance must have limits when faced with movements that exploit democratic freedoms to undermine democracy itself. The paradox challenges liberal political theory: how do you defend openness without becoming closed?

The paradox resurfaces in debates over free speech, platform moderation, religious extremism, and political populism. Philosophers disagree on where to draw the line — between Mill's harm principle, absolute free speech, and proactive defence of democratic institutions.
