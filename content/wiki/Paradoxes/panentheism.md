# Panentheism

<p class="at-lead">
Panentheism holds that the Divine pervades and interpenetrates every part of the universe while also transcending it. Unlike pantheism, which identifies God with the universe, panentheism maintains that God is greater than the sum of all things. It generates two modes of unity — Upper Unity (acosmism) and Lower Unity (creaturely affirmation) — that coexist as a living paradox.
</p>

## Overview

Panentheism bridges classical theism and pantheism by affirming both divine transcendence and intimate presence. In Lurianic Kabbalah and Hasidism, panentheism is the framework within which tzimtzum, acosmism, and immanence are understood as complementary rather than contradictory perspectives. Process theology and some schools of contemporary physics also resonate with panentheistic intuitions.
