# Necktie Paradox

<p class="at-lead">
The Necktie Paradox, also known as the Two Envelopes Problem, is a probability puzzle in which two players compare the value of hidden items (neckties, envelopes, or wallets). Each reasons that they should always exchange, since the expected value of the other's item is higher — a logical impossibility.
</p>

## Overview

Each player has an item of unknown value. Player A reasons: the other player's item is either half or double mine with equal probability, so the expected value of swapping is $1.25\times$ my current value — therefore I should swap. Player B reasons identically. Yet if both swap, nothing changes.

The paradox reveals a flaw in applying expected value to unknown distributions without proper Bayesian priors. Resolutions involve recognising that the "half or double" argument assumes a uniform distribution over all positive reals, which is impossible. The puzzle connects to St. Petersburg-type paradoxes and highlights subtleties in conditional expectation.
