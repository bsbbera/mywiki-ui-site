# Troxler Fading

<p class="at-lead">
Fixate on a central point while a peripheral stimulus remains perfectly static, and the peripheral stimulus gradually fades from awareness entirely. Neural adaptation filters out constant signals so the brain can prioritise change — microsaccades constantly refresh the input to prevent the world from disappearing.
</p>

## Overview

When a stimulus produces unchanging neural firing, receptors and downstream neurons reduce their response rates. The brain evolved to ignore constant signals and prioritise change. Troxler fading is why the world does not disappear when you stare at it: involuntary microsaccades constantly refresh the visual input, resetting adaptation.
