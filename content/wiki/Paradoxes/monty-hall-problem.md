# Monty Hall Problem

<p class="at-lead">
The Monty Hall Problem is a famous probability puzzle based on a game show scenario. A contestant picks one of three doors; behind one is a car, behind the others are goats. After the pick, the host opens a remaining door revealing a goat, then offers a switch. Counterintuitively, switching doubles the chance of winning.
</p>

## Overview

The puzzle is named after the host of *Let's Make a Deal*. The key insight is that the host always reveals a goat and never opens the chosen door — this action conveys information. The initial pick has a 1/3 chance of being correct; the unopened, unchosen door inherits the remaining 2/3 probability.

When Marilyn vos Savant published the correct answer in Parade magazine (1990), she received thousands of letters — including from mathematicians and PhDs — insisting the odds were 50/50. The problem beautifully illustrates how intuitive reasoning fails with conditional probability and remains a staple of statistics education.
