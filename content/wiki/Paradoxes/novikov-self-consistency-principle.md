# Novikov Self-Consistency Principle

<p class="at-lead">
The Novikov Self-Consistency Principle asserts that if time travel is possible, only self-consistent histories can occur. Any action taken by a time traveller must be part of a coherent causal structure — preventing paradoxes by fiat of physical law rather than impossibility.
</p>

## Overview

Proposed by physicist Igor Novikov in the 1980s, the principle addresses the logical problems of closed timelike curves allowed by general relativity. Rather than banning time travel, it says the universe "conspires" to ensure no contradictions arise. A time traveller who tries to kill their grandfather will fail — the gun jams, the aim misses, or the target was never the grandfather.

The principle preserves determinism and unitarity in quantum mechanics, but raises concerns about free will (are failed attempts truly free?) and requires a selection rule for which histories are realised. It influenced science fiction and theoretical work on time-travel paradoxes.
