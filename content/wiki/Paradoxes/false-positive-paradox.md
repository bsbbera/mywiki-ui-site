# False Positive Paradox

<p class="at-lead">
A disease test is 99% accurate, but the disease affects only 1 in 10,000 people. You test positive. Even with a positive result, the odds you actually have the disease remain tiny because the base rate dominates the calculation. Bayes' theorem is essential when interpreting medical tests.
</p>

## Overview

The false positive paradox demonstrates why rare diseases with good tests still yield mostly false positives. If a test has 1% false positive rate and the disease prevalence is 0.01%, then roughly 100 healthy people test positive for every true case. The positive predictive value depends critically on the base rate, not just test accuracy.
