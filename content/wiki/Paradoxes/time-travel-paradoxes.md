# Time Travel Paradoxes

<p class="at-lead">Going back in time generates contradictions that strike at the heart of [[causality]]: if the past can be changed, the present that motivated the journey may never arise, creating logical loops with no clear origin or resolution.</p>

> [!grid]
>> [!card] **Bootstrap Paradox**
>> *Setup:* You send information or an object to your past self, but you only have that information or object because in the past you received it from your future self.
>>
>> *Bite:* The information or object was never created, yet still exists.
>>
>> *Resolution:* No known physical law prevents it; it implies a [[causal-loop|causal loop]] with no identifiable point of origin.
>
>> [!card] **Predestination Paradox**
>> *Setup:* A time traveler journeys to the past to discover the cause of a famous fire, and accidentally knocks over a kerosene lantern, igniting the very blaze that later inspired the trip.
>>
>> *Bite:* The cause of the journey is the journey itself.
>>
>> *Resolution:* Events are self-consistent and predetermined; free will in the past is an illusion.
>
>> [!card] **Temporal Paradox**
>> *Setup:* What happens if a [[time-travel|time traveler]] alters the past in a way that prevents their actions from happening in the first place?
>>
>> *Bite:* The change negates the condition for making the change.
>>
>> *Resolution:* Most frameworks forbid such "grandfather" changes; the universe may be constrained to [[self-consistent|self-consistent]] histories.
>
>> [!card] **Grandfather Paradox**
>> *Setup:* A traveler goes back and kills their grandfather before he conceives a parent, which precludes the traveler's own conception, so they could never go back to kill him.
>>
>> *Bite:* A successful assassination guarantees its own failure.
>>
>> *Resolution:* See deep dive below.
>
>> [!card] **Polchinski's Paradox**
>> *Setup:* A billiard ball is thrown into a wormhole so that it emerges in the past and knocks its incoming past self away from the wormhole entrance.
>>
>> *Bite:* The ball prevents the very trajectory that sent it back.
>>
>> *Resolution:* See deep dive below.
>
>> [!card] **Hitler's Murder Paradox**
>> *Setup:* A traveler goes back to murder Adolf Hitler before World War II and the Holocaust, removing the atrocities that motivated the journey.
>>
>> *Bite:* Success removes the *reason* for the travel.
>>
>> *Resolution:* See deep dive below.

## Grandfather Paradox

The grandfather paradox is the canonical contradiction of [[time-travel|backward time travel]]. If you kill your grandfather before your parent is conceived, you are never born, so you cannot travel back to kill him—so he lives, and you *are* born.

### Self-consistency principle vs Novikov vs many-worlds

- **[[novikov-self-consistency-principle|Novikov self-consistency principle]]:** Igor Novikov proposed that the laws of physics conspire to prevent paradoxes. Any action taken in the past is already part of history; the time traveler *cannot* kill their grandfather because they *didn't*. The universe is constrained to [[self-consistent|self-consistent]] histories.
- **Many-worlds interpretation:** If travel creates a branching [[many-worlds|timeline]], killing "your" grandfather merely strands you in a parallel universe where you were never born, while your original timeline remains intact. No logical contradiction arises because the past you changed is not your own.

## Bootstrap Paradox

Also called the **ontological paradox**, the bootstrap paradox describes a [[causal-loop|causal loop]] in which an object or piece of information has no discernible origin. You receive a notebook from your future self, copy it, and later hand the copy to your past self. The notebook was never manufactured; its existence is sustained entirely by the loop.

## Polchinski's Paradox

Named for physicist Joseph Polchinski, this paradox replaces the human assassin with a billiard ball and a [[wormhole]] time machine. The ball enters the wormhole, emerges in the past, and collides with its younger self, deflecting it away from the entrance so it never enters. The paradox is a physical instantiation of the grandfather paradox: the ball's future existence depends on avoiding the collision that its future self causes.

## Hitler's Murder Paradox

The Hitler's murder paradox is a motivation-removal variant of the grandfather paradox. Instead of preventing your own existence, you prevent the historical evil that motivated the trip. If you succeed, there was no Holocaust or World War II to inspire the invention of time travel and the assassination mission, so no one goes back. But if no one goes back, Hitler lives and the atrocities occur—creating the motivation again. The loop is structurally identical to the grandfather paradox but framed in terms of *motive* rather than *lineage*.
