# Motion-Induced Blindness

<p class="at-lead">
Motion-Induced Blindness (MIB) is a striking visual illusion in which stationary targets disappear from conscious awareness when surrounded by a moving background. The targets are physically present, retinally imaged, and detectable if attention is directed to them — yet they vanish from perception.
</p>

## Overview

First reported by Bonneh, Cooperman, and Sagi in 2001, MIB occurs when a salient moving pattern (e.g., rotating grid) surrounds one or more stationary dots. The dots intermittently disappear and reappear, sometimes for seconds at a time. The effect is robust, involuntary, and resistant to conscious effort to maintain visibility.

MIB is distinct from Troxler fading and relates to competition between neural representations in visual cortex. It reveals that conscious perception is not a direct readout of sensory input but an actively constructed, resource-limited process. MIB has implications for understanding inattentional blindness, change blindness, and the neural correlates of consciousness.
