# Chubb Illusion

<p class="at-lead">
A gray patch surrounded by black appears brighter; the same gray surrounded by white appears darker. The Chubb illusion reveals that the brain computes contrast rather than absolute luminance, using lateral inhibition to enhance edge detection.
</p>

## Overview

Neurons in the retina and primary visual cortex suppress neighbouring regions, so the surround determines the degree of inhibition. We do not see the world as it is, but as differences from local context. The same mechanism underlies the Hermann grid illusion and Mach bands.
