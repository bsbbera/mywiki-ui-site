# Hilbert's Hotel

<p class="at-lead">
Hilbert's Hotel is a thought experiment introduced by mathematician David Hilbert to illustrate the counterintuitive properties of infinite sets. A hotel with infinitely many rooms, all occupied, can still accept new guests — and even infinitely many new guests — through systematic room reassignments.
</p>

## Overview

To accommodate one new guest, every current guest moves from room $n$ to room $n+1$. To accommodate infinitely many new guests, each current guest moves from room $n$ to room $2n$, freeing all odd-numbered rooms. Even infinitely many infinite tour buses can be accommodated by mapping guests to prime-power rooms.

The paradox dramatises that infinite sets can be put in bijection with proper subsets of themselves — a defining property of infinity. It helped popularise Cantor's theory of transfinite numbers and remains a staple introduction to the strange world of infinite cardinalities and countable vs uncountable infinities.
