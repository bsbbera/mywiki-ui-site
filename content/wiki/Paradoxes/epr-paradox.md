# EPR Paradox

<p class="at-lead">
The EPR Paradox, proposed by Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen in 1935, challenges the completeness of quantum mechanics. It argues that if quantum mechanics is complete, then it implies "spooky action at a distance" — now known as quantum entanglement — which Einstein found physically unreasonable.
</p>

## Overview

The EPR thought experiment considers two particles prepared in an entangled state. Measuring one particle's position instantly determines the other's position, no matter the distance. Einstein concluded that quantum mechanics must be incomplete, and "hidden variables" must exist to predetermine outcomes.

John Bell's 1964 theorem and subsequent experiments (Aspect, Zeilinger) have overwhelmingly confirmed that no local hidden-variable theory can reproduce quantum predictions. The EPR paradox thus transformed from a critique into the foundation of quantum information science, teleportation, and quantum cryptography.
