# McGurk Effect

<p class="at-lead">
A video shows a speaker articulating "ga" while the audio plays "ba." The observer perceives "da" — a phoneme that was neither seen nor heard. The McGurk effect demonstrates that audio-visual integration operates at a pre-conscious level, with visual speech information overriding auditory input.
</p>

## Overview

The brain fuses conflicting sensory inputs in the superior temporal sulcus, creating a unified percept that represents a "best guess" rather than either individual signal. The visual component of speech (lip movements) overrides or blends with auditory input, showing that perception is multi-modal and predictive. The effect persists even when the observer knows they are being tricked.
