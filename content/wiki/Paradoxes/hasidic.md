# Hasidic Judaism

<p class="at-lead">
Hasidism is a mystical revival movement within Judaism that emerged in 18th-century Eastern Europe under the Baal Shem Tov. It emphasises direct communion with God through prayer, joy, and the sanctification of everyday life. Hasidic panentheism generates paradoxical modes of unity: Upper Unity sees Creation as illusory (acosmism), while Lower Unity affirms self-aware existence encompassed by the Divine.
</p>

## Overview

Hasidic thought synthesises Lurianic Kabbalah with popular spirituality, making esoteric doctrines accessible through stories, melodies, and personal practice. The tension between divine transcendence and immanence — God is infinitely beyond the world and yet intimately within it — is held as a living paradox rather than a logical contradiction.
