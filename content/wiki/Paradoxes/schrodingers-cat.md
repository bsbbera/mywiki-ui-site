# Schrödinger's Cat

<p class="at-lead">
Erwin Schrödinger's 1935 thought experiment places a cat in a sealed box with a quantum-triggered poison. Until the box is opened, the cat exists in a superposition of alive and dead. The paradox challenges the Copenhagen interpretation by asking when exactly the wavefunction collapses and why macroscopic objects should be exempt from quantum strangeness.
</p>

## Overview

Schrödinger intended the cat as a reductio ad absurdum of applying quantum superposition to everyday objects. Modern decoherence theory explains that the cat's wavefunction entangles with the environment almost instantly, selecting a definite outcome long before an observer opens the box. Yet the broader measurement problem — why we perceive a single result from a branching wavefunction — remains open.
