# Computational Complexity

<p class="at-lead">
Computational Complexity is the study of the resources — time, memory, communication — required to solve computational problems. It classifies problems according to their inherent difficulty, distinguishing tractable instances from intractable ones and guiding algorithm design, cryptography, and systems engineering.
</p>

## Overview

Complexity classes like P (polynomial time), NP (nondeterministic polynomial time), NP-hard, and NP-complete organise problems by their solvability and verifiability. The famous P versus NP question asks whether every problem whose solution can be quickly verified can also be quickly solved. In practice, complexity theory informs when to use exact algorithms, approximation algorithms, or heuristics, and underpins the security of modern cryptography.
