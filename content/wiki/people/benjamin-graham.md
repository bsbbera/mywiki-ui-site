British-American financial analyst, professor, and investor widely regarded as the **father of value investing**. Graham was born in London and raised in New York City. He graduated from Columbia University at 20 and joined a Wall Street bond-trading firm, later founding the **Graham-Newman Corporation** investment fund and teaching at **Columbia Business School** for 28 years.

His core insight — that **Mr. Market** is an emotional bidder, not a rational appraiser — gave rise to the concept of **margin of safety**: buying securities at a significant discount to their intrinsic value to protect against analytical error and market volatility. Every major value investor traces their intellectual lineage to Graham, most famously [[warren-buffett|Warren Buffett]], who called him "the second most influential man in his life after his father."

## Key contributions

- **Security Analysis** (1934, with David Dodd): the foundational text of fundamental analysis; introduced earnings power value and balance-sheet analysis
- **The Intelligent Investor** (1949): the definitive book on defensive and enterprising investing strategies; introduced Mr. Market and Margin of Safety as mental models
- **Margin of Safety principle**: buy only when market price is well below intrinsic value — the single most important concept in value investing
- **Columbia Business School professorship**: trained generations of value investors including Buffett, Walter Schloss, Irving Kahn, and William Ruane

## Interesting facts

- Graham survived the 1929 crash (which wiped out most of his capital) and used the experience to refine and systematize value investing
- Buffett applied to Columbia's graduate program specifically to study under Graham after reading *The Intelligent Investor*
- Graham's final published interview (1976) suggested a simple "stock screen" approach would outperform elaborate analysis — a view that foreshadowed factor investing
