# Dan Linstedt

<p class="at-lead">
American data architect who invented the Data Vault modeling methodology in the 1990s for US federal government systems demanding complete auditability, then evolved it into Data Vault 2.0 — a comprehensive, modern standard that cleanly separates business keys, relationships, and history to enable agile, never-break-the-past enterprise data warehouses.
</p>

<span class="at-kicker">Biography</span>

## Background

American data architect with **25+ years** of experience in data warehousing and business intelligence. Invented the **Data Vault** modeling methodology in the **1990s** while building enterprise data integration systems for the **US federal government** and commercial enterprises — environments that demanded complete auditability, traceability, and adaptability to changing business requirements.

Published the first formal version of Data Vault in the **early 2000s**. The model uses three entity types — **Hubs** (business keys), **Links** (relationships between hubs), and **Satellites** (descriptive context and history) — to separate concerns cleanly, enabling agile schema evolution without rewriting existing structures. In **2012**, announced **Data Vault 2.0**, which was fully released in **2013** as an expanded, modernized methodology integrating big data, NoSQL patterns, automation, and Agile/DevOps delivery practices.

Founder and CEO of **DataVaultAlliance** and co-founder of **Scalefree International**. Organized the inaugural **International Data Vault Consortium** (2014). Holds certifications including CBIP Certified Master, DW2.0 Certified Architect, and Informatica Certified Master.

## Key contributions

> [!grid|cols3]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ### Data Vault 1.0 (1990s)
>> Hub/Link/Satellite model for **auditable, agile** enterprise data warehousing — invented for US federal government systems requiring complete auditability where every fact must be traceable to its source and time of arrival.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ### Data Vault 2.0 (2013)
>> Full methodology and architecture incorporating **big data, NoSQL patterns, automation, and Agile/DevOps** delivery practices — one of the few traditional modeling methodologies to explicitly accommodate modern lakehouse architectures.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ### DataVaultAlliance
>> Certification, training, and practitioner community for the global Data Vault ecosystem — providing structured learning paths and certifications that standardize Data Vault implementation across organizations.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ### International Data Vault Consortium (2014)
>> Organized the inaugural consortium to coordinate global standards, education, and certification for Data Vault methodology — establishing a formal governance body for the methodology's continued evolution.

## Interesting facts

> [!grid|cols2]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ### Born from Federal Auditability
>> Data Vault was originally designed for **US federal government** systems requiring complete auditability — where every fact must be traceable to its source and time of arrival. This origin explains its emphasis on never deleting or altering historical records.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ### Schema Changes Never Break History
>> The methodology's separation of business keys (Hubs) from descriptive attributes (Satellites) means **schema changes never break historical data** — a critical property for long-lived enterprise systems that must evolve without rewriting the past.
