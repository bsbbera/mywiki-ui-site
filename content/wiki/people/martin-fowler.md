British-American software developer and author, **Chief Scientist at ThoughtWorks**. Born in **Walsall, England**, educated at Queen Mary's Grammar School and **University College London** (BSc Electronic Engineering & Computer Science, 1986). Moved to the United States in **1994** and is based near **Boston, Massachusetts**.

One of the **17 original signatories of the Agile Manifesto** (2001). Published *Refactoring: Improving the Design of Existing Code* (1999) — the canonical text that made systematic code refactoring a named, teachable practice. His website **martinfowler.com** is one of the most-referenced catalogues of software architecture patterns in existence.

Articulated or popularized a remarkable number of foundational architectural patterns: **Event Sourcing**, the **Strangler Fig Pattern** (the standard approach for incrementally migrating monoliths to microservices), **Presentation Model**, **CQRS** (working with [[greg-young|Greg Young]]), **Microservices** (2014 article co-authored with James Lewis), and Data Mesh essays written with [[zhamak-dehghani|Zhamak Dehghani]]. Books include *Patterns of Enterprise Application Architecture* (PoEAA), *NoSQL Distilled*, *UML Distilled*, *Domain-Specific Languages*, and *Refactoring* (2nd ed., 2018).

## Key contributions

- **Refactoring** (1999): codified the practice of improving code structure without changing behavior
- **Agile Manifesto** (2001): one of 17 original signatories
- **Patterns of Enterprise Application Architecture** (2002): catalogue of enterprise integration patterns
- **Strangler Fig Pattern**: the standard method for incrementally replacing a legacy system
- **Microservices** (2014, with James Lewis): the article that named and popularized the microservices architectural style
- **martinfowler.com**: living reference catalogue of software patterns

## Interesting facts

- The **Strangler Fig** pattern was inspired by a botanical phenomenon — the strangler fig tree that grows around and eventually replaces the host tree — an elegant metaphor for incrementally replacing a legacy monolith
- Martin Fowler was among the first to write comprehensively about **Data Mesh** — amplifying Zhamak Dehghani's work to a global engineering audience
- His *Refactoring* (1st edition, 1999) was written in close collaboration with **Kent Beck** and features examples that shaped how an entire generation of developers thinks about clean code
