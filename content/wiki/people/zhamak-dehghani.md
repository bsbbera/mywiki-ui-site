Iranian software architect and technologist, former **Director of Emerging Technologies at ThoughtWorks North America**. Holds a **Bachelor's degree in Electrical Engineering**. Introduced the concept of **Data Mesh** in **2018–2019** through a series of influential blog posts on ThoughtWorks Insights, framing it as a sociotechnical response to a widespread failure pattern: centralized data lakes and monolithic data platforms become bottlenecks at enterprise scale, creating data quality issues, slow time-to-insight, and organizational friction as data ownership is separated from the domain teams who understand the data best.

**Data Mesh** rests on four core principles:
1. **Domain-oriented decentralized data ownership** — data is owned by the domain teams that produce it, not a central data team
2. **Data as a product** — each domain treats its data outputs as first-class products with defined SLAs, discoverability, and quality guarantees
3. **Self-serve data infrastructure as a platform** — a platform team provides tooling that enables domain teams to manage their data products autonomously
4. **Federated computational governance** — global interoperability standards and policies applied locally by each domain

Published as the book **Data Mesh: Delivering Data-Driven Value at Scale** (O'Reilly, 2022). Founded **Nextdata** — a software company building a scalable, federated platform for **Autonomous Data Products**, operationalizing the Data Mesh architecture in enterprise environments.

## Key contributions

- **Data Mesh paradigm** (2018/2019): four principles for decentralized, domain-owned analytical data
- *Data Mesh* book (O'Reilly, 2022): the definitive guide to implementing Data Mesh
- **Nextdata**: enterprise platform for autonomous data products
- Synthesized lessons from distributed systems, DDD, and platform engineering into a data architecture paradigm

## Interesting facts

- Data Mesh was a direct response to failure patterns Dehghani observed at large enterprises trying to centralize analytics — where the central data team became a permanent bottleneck and data quality suffered because domain knowledge was separated from data ownership
- The **"data as a product"** principle borrowed product engineering disciplines (SLAs, versioning, discoverability, quality contracts) and applied them to analytical data — reframing data from a byproduct of operations into a first-class deliverable
- Data Mesh is as much a **sociotechnical and organizational** concept as a technical one — its adoption requires restructuring team ownership, not just changing tooling
