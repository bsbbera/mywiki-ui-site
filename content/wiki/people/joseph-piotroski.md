> # Joseph Piotroski
> ###### Accounting Professor & Researcher

American accounting professor at the **Stanford Graduate School of Business**, best known for developing the **Piotroski F-Score** — a 9-point financial signal score introduced in his 2000 paper *Value Investing: The Use of Historical Financial Statement Information to Separate Winners from Losers*.

The paper demonstrated that among value stocks (those trading at low price-to-book ratios), a simple set of nine accounting-based signals could distinguish future winners from losers and generate significant abnormal returns. The nine signals are organized into three buckets: **profitability** (4 signals), **leverage/liquidity/source of funds** (3 signals), and **operating efficiency** (2 signals).

Before joining Stanford in 2007, Piotroski was a professor at the **University of Chicago Booth School of Business**.

## Key contributions

- **Piotroski F-Score** (2000): 9-point binary scoring system using accounting ratios; F-score 8–9 = financially strong; 0–2 = financially weak; used by quantitative value investors worldwide
- Research on the information content of financial statements in predicting stock returns
- Contribution to the literature on fundamental analysis vs. market efficiency

## Interesting facts

- The F-Score paper was originally academic research, not intended as an investment tool — it became widely adopted by practitioners after being popularized by value investing bloggers in the 2000s
- Studies replicating Piotroski's backtests have confirmed positive abnormal returns across multiple markets and time periods
- The F-Score is now a standard screener variable on Bloomberg Terminal, Morningstar, and most quant factor databases
