American author, researcher, and entrepreneur who has studied high-performing technology organizations for over 25 years. Kim founded **Tripwire Inc** (enterprise security software) in 1999 and served as CTO for 13 years. He then pivoted to research and writing, becoming one of the most influential voices in the **DevOps movement**.

His body of work spans narrative business novels, empirical research reports (the annual **State of DevOps** report, co-authored for years), and practical handbooks — each aimed at helping technology and business leaders understand what makes engineering organizations thrive.

## Key contributions

- **[[../books/the-phoenix-project|The Phoenix Project]]** (2013, with Kevin Behr & George Spafford): the defining novel about DevOps — follows an IT manager at a fictional manufacturer battling dysfunctional processes; introduced "The Three Ways" of DevOps (Flow, Feedback, Continuous Learning)
- **[[../books/accelerate|Accelerate]]** (2018, with Nicole Forsgren & [[jez-humble|Jez Humble]]): evidence-based research into software delivery performance; introduced DORA metrics (Deployment Frequency, Lead Time for Changes, Change Failure Rate, Time to Restore)
- **The DevOps Handbook** (2016): practical implementation guide for DevOps transformation
- **Tripwire**: early enterprise file-integrity monitoring solution, later acquired by Belden

## Interesting facts

- The annual **State of DevOps Report** (which Kim co-authored in its early years) surveyed over 30,000 technology professionals and became the most data-rich longitudinal study of software delivery
- The DORA metrics introduced in *Accelerate* are now standard KPIs used by engineering leadership at Google, Microsoft, Amazon, and thousands of other companies
- *The Phoenix Project* uses the format of *The Goal* by Eliyahu Goldratt (the TOC / Theory of Constraints novel) as a storytelling template
