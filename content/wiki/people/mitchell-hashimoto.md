> # Mitchell Hashimoto
> ###### Entrepreneur & Software Engineer
> | | |
> | --- | --- |
> | **Nationality** | American |
> | **Domain** | Infrastructure as Code, DevOps tooling, cloud infrastructure |
> | **Known for** | Founder of HashiCorp; creator of [[../technology/software-engineering/terraform\|Terraform]], Vagrant, Packer, Vault, Consul, Nomad |
> | **Notable works** | Terraform, Vagrant, Packer, Vault, Consul, Nomad |
> | **Institution** | HashiCorp (co-founder); University of Washington (CS, attended) |

American software engineer and entrepreneur who co-founded **HashiCorp** with Armon Dadgar in 2012 while studying at the University of Washington. HashiCorp became the company behind the most widely adopted **Infrastructure as Code** tools in the industry. Hashimoto is the primary creator of:

- **Vagrant** (2010) — developer environment management that brought reproducible dev boxes to the mainstream
- **Packer** (2013) — machine image automation across cloud providers
- **[[../technology/software-engineering/terraform|Terraform]]** (2014) — declarative cloud-agnostic infrastructure provisioning; now the de-facto IaC standard
- **Vault** — secrets management
- **Consul** — service mesh and service discovery
- **Nomad** — workload orchestration

Hashimoto stepped back from day-to-day operations at HashiCorp in 2023 after the company was acquired by IBM for $6.4B. He has since returned to individual open-source contribution and software craftsmanship.

## Key contributions

- **Terraform** (2014): declarative HCL-based IaC tool that works across AWS, GCP, Azure, and 300+ providers; adopted by virtually every major tech company
- **Vagrant** (2010): pioneered the concept of developer environments as code, long before Docker made containers mainstream
- **HashiCorp product suite**: a coherent set of tools covering the entire infrastructure lifecycle (provision → secure → connect → run)

## Interesting facts

- Hashimoto started Vagrant as a side project while in college; it went viral among developers before he founded HashiCorp
- The BSD 1.1 re-licensing of Terraform in 2023 (from Mozilla Public License) sparked controversy and led to the community fork **OpenTofu** (now a Linux Foundation project)
- HashiCorp's $6.4B acquisition by IBM (2024) is one of the largest open-source tooling exits in history
