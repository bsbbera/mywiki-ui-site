American investor and philanthropist widely regarded as the greatest capital allocator in history. Buffett began investing at age 11 and filed his first tax return at 13. After studying under [[benjamin-graham|Benjamin Graham]] at Columbia Business School — the man who invented value investing — he formed the **Buffett Partnership** in 1956 and later took control of **Berkshire Hathaway** (1965), transforming it from a failing textile company into one of the world's largest conglomerates.

Buffett's investment philosophy combines Graham's **margin of safety** with [[charlie-munger|Charlie Munger]]'s emphasis on buying *wonderful companies at fair prices* rather than fair companies at wonderful prices. He is famous for concentrating on businesses with durable **competitive moats**, strong free cash flow, and capable management — and then holding them forever.

## Key contributions

- **Value investing popularization**: made Graham's principles accessible to mainstream investors through decades of plain-language shareholder letters
- **Capital allocation**: demonstrated that long-term compounding (20.1% CAGR from 1965 to 2023) dwarfs short-term trading
- **Insurance float**: pioneered using insurance premiums as free leverage to invest long-term
- **Philanthropist**: pledged 99% of wealth to the [[the-bill-and-melinda-gates-foundation|Gates Foundation]] and family foundations

## Interesting facts

- Buffett's net worth grew from ~$1M at age 30 to over $100B — 99% of it after age 50, illustrating the power of compounding
- He still lives in the same Omaha house he bought in 1958 for $31,500
- His annual Berkshire shareholder letters are studied as masterclasses in business thinking and plain communication
