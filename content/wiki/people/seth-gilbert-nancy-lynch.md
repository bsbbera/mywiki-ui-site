> # Seth Gilbert & Nancy Lynch
> ###### Distributed Systems Researchers
> | | |
> | --- | --- |
> | **Members** | Seth Gilbert, Nancy Lynch |
> | **Born** | 19 January 1948 (Lynch) |
> | **Nationality** | American |
> | **Domain** | Distributed computing theory, formal methods |
> | **Known for** | Formal proof of the CAP theorem (2002); FLP impossibility (Lynch, 1985) |
> | **Awards** | Dijkstra Prize in Distributed Computing (2001, Lynch); Dijkstra Prize (2004, Lynch+Gilbert) |
> | **Institution** | Nancy Lynch: MIT (NEC Professor of Software Science & Engineering, CSAIL); Seth Gilbert: National University of Singapore (Professor) |

MIT researchers who **formally proved** the CAP theorem in 2002 in the paper *"Brewer's Conjecture and the Feasibility of Consistent, Available, Partition-Tolerant Web Services"* — turning [[eric-brewer|Eric Brewer]]'s 2000 keynote conjecture into a rigorous mathematical theorem and providing the distributed systems community with a formal foundation for reasoning about trade-offs in networked storage systems.

**Nancy Lynch** (born January 19, 1948) is the **NEC Professor of Software Science and Engineering** in MIT's EECS department and heads the **Theory of Distributed Systems** research group at **MIT CSAIL**. Her contributions to distributed computing theory span four decades and extend far beyond CAP. Most famously: the **FLP impossibility result** (1985, with Fischer and Paterson) — proving that no deterministic protocol can achieve **consensus** in an asynchronous distributed system if even one process might fail. This result, published as *"Impossibility of Distributed Consensus with One Faulty Process"*, won the **ACM Dijkstra Prize in 2001** — 16 years after publication, a testament to its foundational importance. Author of the graduate textbook **Distributed Algorithms** — considered the theoretical bible of the field.

**Seth Gilbert** is currently a **Professor at the National University of Singapore**, working on distributed algorithms, wireless networks, and data management.

## Key contributions

- **CAP theorem proof** (2002): formalized Brewer's conjecture into a theorem with rigorous proof
- **FLP impossibility result** (Lynch, 1985): consensus is impossible with even one faulty process in an asynchronous system
- **Distributed Algorithms** textbook (Lynch): the authoritative graduate reference on distributed computing theory

## Interesting facts

- The **FLP impossibility result** earned the Dijkstra Prize **16 years** after publication — a measure of how long it took the field to fully recognize its depth
- The CAP theorem is often taught alongside PACELC (by [[daniel-abadi|Daniel Abadi]]) in modern distributed systems courses — CAP describes the partition-time trade-off; PACELC describes the always-present latency vs consistency trade-off
- Nancy Lynch's research group at MIT has produced some of the most foundational formal results in distributed computing, including work on consensus, fault tolerance, and timed automata
