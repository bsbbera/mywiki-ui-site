# Accelerate

<p class="at-lead">
<em>Accelerate</em> is the first rigorous, statistically grounded study of what makes software delivery organizations high-performing. Based on four years of research surveying 23,000+ technology professionals, Nicole Forsgren, Jez Humble, and Gene Kim prove that software delivery performance drives organizational performance — and identify exactly what drives it.
</p>

<span class="at-stat">23,000+</span> survey respondents &nbsp;·&nbsp; <span class="at-stat">4 DORA metrics</span> &nbsp;·&nbsp; <span class="at-mark">the evidence base for every DevOps transformation</span>

## The DORA Metrics

> [!grid|cols2]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### THROUGHPUT
>> ### Deployment Frequency & Lead Time
>> **Deployment Frequency** — how often code is deployed to production. **Lead Time for Changes** — time from code committed to code in production. High performers deploy on demand (multiple times/day) with lead times under one hour; low performers deploy monthly and take months to deliver changes.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### STABILITY
>> ### Change Failure Rate & Time to Restore
>> **Change Failure Rate** — percentage of deployments causing a failure in production. **Time to Restore Service** — how long to recover from a production incident. High performers have <15% change failure rate and restore in under one hour.

## Key Findings

- Software delivery performance **positively predicts** organizational performance (profitability, market share, productivity) — not just IT metrics
- **Lean management and continuous delivery practices** are the strongest predictors of high performance — not team size, technology choice, or budget
- **Westrum organizational culture** (generative vs. bureaucratic vs. pathological) is both a predictor of software delivery performance and an outcome of good technical practices
- High performers are **2× more likely to exceed profitability, market share, and productivity goals** than low performers
- Continuous delivery, trunk-based development, test automation, and shift-left security are the highest-leverage technical practices

## Interesting facts

- The research used **Structural Equation Modelling** — a sophisticated statistical technique — to prove causality, not just correlation, between technical practices and business outcomes
- Nicole Forsgren (the lead researcher) has a PhD in Management Information Systems, which gave the research its statistical rigour unusual in the DevOps space
- DORA was acquired by Google in 2018; the annual State of DevOps Report continues under Google Cloud
