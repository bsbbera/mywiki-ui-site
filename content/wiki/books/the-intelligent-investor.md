# The Intelligent Investor

<p class="at-lead">
First published in 1949, <em>The Intelligent Investor</em> is the definitive book on value investing — the philosophy of buying stocks at prices below their intrinsic value and holding them with the discipline of a business owner, not a speculator. Warren Buffett called it "by far the best book on investing ever written."
</p>

<span class="at-stat">1949</span> first edition &nbsp;·&nbsp; <span class="at-stat">74 years</span> in print &nbsp;·&nbsp; <span class="at-mark">the bible of value investing</span>

## Core Concepts

> [!grid|cols3]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### MR. MARKET
>> ### The Mr. Market Allegory
>> Graham introduced Mr. Market — an imaginary business partner who every day offers to buy or sell his share at a different price, sometimes euphoric, sometimes depressed. The intelligent investor exploits this irrationality rather than being swept by it.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### MARGIN OF SAFETY
>> ### Margin of Safety
>> The central concept of the book: always buy securities at a significant discount to their intrinsic value. This discount is the "margin of safety" — a buffer against analytical errors, market declines, and unforeseen events.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### DEFENSIVE VS ENTERPRISING
>> ### Two Investor Types
>> Graham distinguishes the *defensive* investor (passive, wants to avoid serious mistakes) from the *enterprising* investor (active, willing to do the work). The book provides separate strategies for each type, both grounded in value principles.

## Key Takeaways

- **Price vs. Value**: the market price and intrinsic value of a security are different things. The gap between them is your opportunity.
- **Inflation**: Graham warned about the risk of inflation eroding purchasing power — bonds alone are not safe long-term.
- **Dollar-cost averaging**: for the defensive investor, regular investing in an index removes the need to time the market.
- **Diversification**: the enterprising investor should hold 10–30 securities across industries to diversify specific risk without over-diversifying into mediocrity.
- **Avoid speculation**: speculating (buying based on price movement hope) dressed up as investing is the most dangerous trap.

## Interesting facts

- [[../people/warren-buffett|Warren Buffett]] first read *The Intelligent Investor* at age 19 and called it "the best book on investing ever written"
- The 1973 revised edition is the most widely read; Jason Zweig's 2003 annotated edition adds contemporary market commentary to each chapter
- The "margin of safety" concept from this book became the title of Seth Klarman's legendary 1991 hedge fund manual
