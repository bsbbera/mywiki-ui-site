# Interview Techniques

<p class="at-lead">
Technical skills get you the interview; communication skills get you the offer. Behavioural
interviews, structured storytelling, and strategic negotiation are the differentiators that
separate candidates who are "qualified" from candidates who are hired. Mastering the STAR
format, anticipating common questions, and negotiating from a position of strength are
learnable skills — not innate talents.
</p>

<span class="at-kicker">STAR Format</span>

## Structured Storytelling

The **STAR** method is the industry-standard framework for answering behavioural interview
questions. It ensures your answers are concise, complete, and memorable.

| Letter | Element | What to include |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| **S** | Situation | Set the scene — the project, team, stakes, and constraints |
| **T** | Task | Your specific responsibility or challenge in that situation |
| **A** | Action | The exact steps you took — focus on "I," not "we" |
| **R** | Result | Quantified outcome, lessons learned, and follow-on impact |

> [!example] STAR in action
> **Q**: "Tell me about a time you had to meet a tight deadline."
>
> **S**: "We had a quarterly board presentation scheduled, and the data pipeline broke 48
> hours before the deadline."
>
> **T**: "I was the senior data engineer responsible for delivering the executive dashboard."
>
> **A**: "I diagnosed the failure as a schema drift issue, wrote a hotfix migration script,
> coordinated with the analytics team to validate downstream tables, and set up a staging
> environment to test the fix before production."
>
> **R**: "We delivered the dashboard 6 hours early. The fix also became our standard schema
> validation check, preventing three similar incidents that quarter."

> [!tip] Quantify everything
> Numbers make stories credible. "Reduced latency by 40%" is better than "improved performance."
> "Managed a team of 5" is better than "led a team."

## Behavioural Question Bank

### Pressure & conflict

| Question | What they are testing | Good angle |
|----------|----------------------|------------|
| "Tell me about a time your work was criticised." | Coachability, resilience | Show you sought feedback, improved, and delivered |
| "How do you handle stress?" | Emotional regulation | Describe a process: pause → prioritise → execute |
| "Describe a conflict with a coworker." | Communication, empathy | Focus on resolution, not blame |
| "How did you handle a heavy workload?" | Prioritisation, delegation | Mention Eisenhower Matrix, manager alignment |

### Motivation & fit

| Question | What they are testing | Good angle |
|----------|----------------------|------------|
| "Why do you want to leave your current role?" | Loyalty, ambition | Frame as growth seeking, not escape |
| "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" | Ambition, realism | Align with company trajectory |
| "What is your greatest weakness?" | Self-awareness | Genuine weakness + active remediation |
| "How do you evaluate success?" | Values alignment | Metrics + feedback + team impact |

> [!warning] Avoid these traps
> - Bad-mouthing previous employers or managers
> - Claiming to have no weaknesses ("I work too hard" is a cliché)
> - Vague answers without concrete examples
> - Overusing "we" — interviewers want to know what *you* did

## Getting What You Are Worth

### The golden rules

1. **Never give a number first**
   - If pressed: "I am confident you will make a fair offer based on my experience."
   - Deflect to range research: "Based on my research, the market rate for this role is
     $X–$Y. I am open to discussing the total package."

2. **Timing matters**
   - Your leverage increases as you progress through interviews.
   - Do not negotiate before you have an offer in hand.

3. **Negotiate the total package**
   - Base salary, signing bonus, equity, benefits, remote flexibility, professional development
     budget, and title are all negotiable.

4. **Have alternatives**
   - The strongest negotiating position is a competing offer.
   - Even without one, frame the decision as "I am evaluating multiple opportunities."

> [!tip] The anchoring effect
> Whoever names a number first anchors the negotiation. If you must give a range, make the
> bottom of your range higher than your actual minimum. Research Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, and
> PayScale for market data.

### Post-offer script

> "Thank you for the offer. I am very excited about the role and the team. Based on my
> research and the value I believe I can add — particularly in [specific area] — I was
> hoping for a base closer to $X. Is there flexibility in the package?"

## Body Language & Presence

| Technique | Why It Works |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Mirroring** | Subtly match the interviewer's posture and pace to build rapport |
| **Power poses** | Open posture (uncrossed arms, upright spine) projects confidence |
| **Pause before answering** | Shows thoughtfulness; prevents rambling |
| **Ask clarifying questions** | Demonstrates engagement and precision |
| **Thank-you email** | Sent within 24 hours; references specific conversation points |

### The 30-60-90 day plan

When asked "How long until you make a significant contribution?", present a structured plan:

| Phase | Focus | Deliverables |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| **Days 1–30** | Learn | Onboarding, stakeholder mapping, codebase audit |
| **Days 31–60** | Contribute | First independent deliverable, process improvement proposal |
| **Days 61–90** | Own | Full ownership of a workstream, mentoring or documentation |

> [!info] Reverse interviewing
> Ask the interviewer:
> - "What does success look like in this role at 6 months?"
> - "What is the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"
> - "How do you evaluate performance?"
>
> This signals confidence and shifts the dynamic from interrogation to conversation.

## Interesting facts

- Studies show that candidates who negotiate their starting salary increase lifetime earnings
  by $600K–$1M on average.
- The "likability" bias in interviews is strong — interviewers hire people they would enjoy
  working with, even when technical scores are lower. Warmth + competence = hire.
- Amazon's Leadership Principles interview explicitly forbids hypothetical answers — every
  response must be a specific past example with metrics.

## Interview questions

1. Describe a situation where you had to deliver bad news to a stakeholder. How did you handle it?
2. How would you answer "What is your greatest weakness?" in a way that is honest but not damaging?
3. What is the STAR format, and why does it work?
4. You receive an offer 15% below your target. Walk through your negotiation approach.
5. How do you establish credibility quickly when joining a new team?
