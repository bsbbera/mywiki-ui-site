# Macroeconomics

<p class="at-lead">
Macroeconomics studies the behaviour of aggregate variables — GDP, unemployment, inflation, interest rates, and trade balances — and the policies that influence them. It spans short-run business-cycle analysis (Keynesian and monetarist traditions) and long-run growth theory (Solow, endogenous growth, and institutional models).
</p>

## Overview

Central questions include what causes recessions, how monetary and fiscal policy affect output, and why some countries grow faster than others. Paradoxes such as the paradox of thrift and the Jevons paradox show that individual rationality does not guarantee collective optimality. Modern macroeconomics increasingly incorporates behavioural foundations and heterogenous-agent models.
