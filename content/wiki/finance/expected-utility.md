# Expected Utility

<p class="at-lead">
Expected utility theory defines rational choice as maximising the weighted average of utility, where weights are probabilities. Under axioms of completeness, transitivity, continuity, and independence, preferences can be represented by a utility function. The Allais paradox and Ellsberg paradox show that real human preferences systematically violate the independence axiom.
</p>

## Overview

Developed by von Neumann and Morgenstern in the 1940s, expected utility became the bedrock of microeconomics, finance, and game theory. Risk aversion is modelled as concave utility — the certainty equivalent of a gamble is less than its expected value. Alternative models such as prospect theory relax the independence axiom to fit empirical behaviour.
