# Information Economics

<p class="at-lead">
Information economics studies how information asymmetries — where one party knows more than another — shape markets, contracts, and institutions. Founded by Kenneth Arrow, George Akerlof, Michael Spence, and Joseph Stiglitz, the field explains why markets can fail, why signalling and screening matter, and how the Arrow information paradox undermines direct sales of knowledge.
</p>

## Overview

Key insights include Akerlof's "market for lemons" (asymmetric quality drives out good products), Spence's signalling theory (education as a costly signal of ability), and Stiglitz's screening models. Information economics justifies intellectual property, warranties, reputation systems, and vertical integration as institutional responses to information gaps.
