# Finance

<p class="at-lead">
Finance is the science and art of managing money — understanding how businesses record and report their economics, how analysts measure performance with ratios, and how investors determine what a company is truly worth. This knowledge base covers the three pillars: financial statements, financial ratios, and investing & valuation.
</p>

## The Three Core Statements

Every company's financial health is captured in three statements. Together they answer: *How profitable? How solvent? How liquid?*

> [!grid|cols3]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### INCOME STATEMENT
>> ### *Income* Statement
>> Reports revenue, costs, and profits over a period. The P&L shows how much the company earned after all expenses — from COGS to taxes.
>> → [[financial-statements/income-statement|Income Statement]]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### BALANCE SHEET
>> ### *Balance* Sheet
>> A snapshot of assets, liabilities, and shareholders' equity at a specific date. Anchored by: **Assets = Liabilities + Equity**.
>> → [[financial-statements/balance-sheet|Balance Sheet]]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### CASH FLOW
>> ### *Cash Flow* Statement
>> Tracks the actual movement of cash through operating, investing, and financing activities. Reveals the true liquidity behind the profit numbers.
>> → [[financial-statements/cash-flow-statement|Cash Flow Statement]]

## Essential Formulas

| Concept | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| **Gross Profit** | Revenue − COGS |
| **EBITDA** | Operating Revenue − Operating Expense |
| **Operating Income** | Gross Profit − Operating Expenses |
| **Net Income (PAT)** | PBT − Taxes |
| **Free Cash Flow** | Operating Cash Flow − CapEx |
| **ROE** | Net Profit / Shareholders' Equity × 100 |
| **ROA** | (Net Income + Interest × (1−Tax)) / Avg Total Assets |
| **ROCE** | EBIT / Capital Employed |
| **P/E Ratio** | Share Price / EPS |
| **DCF Intrinsic Value** | PV of all future free cash flows |

> [!tip] Where to Start
> New to finance? Start with [[financial-statements/income-statement|the Income Statement]] to understand how a company makes money, then [[financial-statements/balance-sheet|the Balance Sheet]] to understand what it owns and owes, then [[financial-statements/cash-flow-statement|Cash Flow]] to understand real liquidity. Only after that do [[financial-ratios/financial-ratios|ratios]] start to make full sense.

> [!info] How Statements Connect
> The P&L's net income flows into Shareholders' Equity on the Balance Sheet as retained earnings. The Cash Flow Statement's ending cash balance appears on the Balance Sheet as cash & equivalents. Depreciation from the P&L reduces asset values on the Balance Sheet. All three are inseparable.
