> [!quote]
> *An allocation is efficient if the winners could in principle compensate the losers.*
> — Nicholas Kaldor & John Hicks

# Kaldor–Hicks Efficiency

<p class="at-lead">
An outcome is Kaldor–Hicks efficient if the total gains exceed the total losses, so that the winners could hypothetically compensate the losers and still be better off. It is weaker than Pareto efficiency — no actual compensation is required — and is the standard criterion used in cost–benefit analysis and policy evaluation.
</p>

## Overview

The Kaldor–Hicks criterion justifies many policy changes that create both winners and losers, from free-trade agreements to infrastructure projects. However, it can produce cycles: allocation A may be more efficient than B while B is more efficient than A. The Scitovsky paradox showed that both directions of compensation must be possible for a robust efficiency ranking.
