# Keynesian Multiplier

<p class="at-lead">
The Keynesian multiplier measures how much total income increases in response to an initial injection of spending. If the marginal propensity to consume is 0.8, a £1 increase in government spending ultimately raises GDP by £5. The multiplier underpins the paradox of thrift: saving is virtuous for an individual but potentially lethal for an economy in recession.
</p>

## Overview

Keynes introduced the multiplier during the Great Depression to justify deficit-financed public works. The size of the multiplier depends on leakages — taxes, imports, and saving — and on whether the economy is at full employment. Modern empirical estimates vary widely, from near-zero in highly open economies with flexible exchange rates to above 1.5 during deep recessions with binding zero lower bounds.
