# Cost-Benefit Analysis

<p class="at-lead">
Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA) is a systematic process for evaluating the merits of a project, policy, or decision by comparing the total expected costs against the total expected benefits, expressed in monetary terms. It is the standard tool for public policy evaluation and capital budgeting.
</p>

## Overview

CBA involves identifying all stakeholders, quantifying impacts (direct, indirect, tangible, intangible), discounting future cash flows to present value, and computing metrics like **Net Present Value (NPV)**, **Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR)**, and **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)**. Sensitivity analysis tests robustness to assumptions.

Challenges include valuing non-market goods (environment, health, life), choosing the social discount rate, distributional concerns (who bears costs vs who receives benefits), and political manipulation of estimates. Despite limitations, CBA imposes discipline on decision-making and reveals trade-offs.
