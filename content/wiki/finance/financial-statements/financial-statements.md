# Financial Statements

<p class="at-lead">
Financial statements are the formal records of a company's financial activities — three interconnected reports that together answer every fundamental question about a business: Is it profitable? Is it solvent? Does it generate real cash?
</p>

<span class="at-stat">3 statements</span> that define a business &nbsp;·&nbsp; <span class="at-stat">P&L · Balance Sheet · Cash Flow</span> &nbsp;·&nbsp; <span class="at-mark">the foundation of all fundamental analysis</span>

<span class="at-kicker">The Three Reports</span>

## The Three Core Statements

> [!grid|cols3]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### INCOME STATEMENT
>> ### *Income* Statement
>> Reports revenue, expenses, and profit over a specific **period** (quarter or year). Shows the journey from top-line revenue to bottom-line net income.
>> → [[income-statement|Full Income Statement Guide]]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### BALANCE SHEET
>> ### *Balance* Sheet
>> A **snapshot** at a specific date showing what the company owns (assets), owes (liabilities), and the residual belonging to shareholders (equity). The fundamental equation: **Assets = Liabilities + Equity**.
>> → [[balance-sheet|Full Balance Sheet Guide]]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### CASH FLOW
>> ### *Cash Flow* Statement
>> Tracks actual cash movement through **operating**, **investing**, and **financing** activities. Reveals liquidity that pure profit numbers can hide.
>> → [[cash-flow-statement|Full Cash Flow Guide]]

<span class="at-kicker">The Annual Report</span>

## Reading an Annual Report

An Annual Report (AR) is filed at end of each financial year. Its key sections:

| Section | What It Contains |
| --- | --- |
| **Management Statement** | CEO perspective on business positioning and industry outlook |
| **MD&A** (Management Discussion & Analysis) | Macro trends, economic activity, business sentiment — the most important analytical section |
| **Human Resources Report** | Manpower and employee-related points |
| **R&D Report** | Research and development initiatives |
| **Technology Report** | Technology investments and direction |
| **Financial Report** | The three core statements: P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow |

> [!info] Consolidated vs. Standalone
> Financial statements come in two forms. **Standalone** statements reflect only the parent company without subsidiaries. **Consolidated** statements include all subsidiaries. When a company has multiple subsidiaries, consolidated numbers give a fuller picture. When you want to isolate parent performance, standalone numbers are more useful.

<span class="at-kicker">How They Connect</span>

## Statement Interconnections

The three statements are not independent — they are deeply linked:

| P&L Statement | Connects to Balance Sheet |
| --- | --- |
| Sales Revenue | Receivables and Cash Balance |
| Operating Expense | Inventory and Trade Payables |
| Depreciation & Amortization | Accumulated Depreciation |
| Other Income | Investments |
| Finance Cost | Debt |
| PAT (Net Income) | Shareholders' Equity (Retained Earnings) |

> [!tip] The Flow of Numbers
> - The P&L's **net income** (PAT) flows into the Balance Sheet as **retained earnings** under Shareholders' Equity
> - The Balance Sheet's **cash & cash equivalents** is sourced from the **ending balance** of the Cash Flow Statement
> - **Depreciation** mentioned in the P&L is carried forward to reduce asset values on the Balance Sheet
> - The Cash Flow Statement **starts with net income** from the P&L and adjusts for non-cash items

> [!warning] Key Distinctions
> - The **Balance Sheet** is on a **flow basis** — it accumulates data from the day the company was incorporated
> - The **P&L** and **Cash Flow Statement** are prepared on a **standalone basis** — representing only the given year's financial position
> - A company can be profitable on the P&L but cash-flow negative — always check both

<span class="at-kicker">What to Look For</span>

## Analytical Framework

When analysing financial statements, follow this sequence:

> [!grid|cols3]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### STEP 1 — PROFITABILITY
>> ### *Profitability* Check
>> Start with the P&L. Is revenue growing? Are gross margins consistent? Is net income growing in line with revenue? Watch for revenue growth without profit growth — a red flag.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### STEP 2 — SOLVENCY
>> ### *Solvency* Check
>> Move to the Balance Sheet. Is the debt level manageable? Can earnings repay long-term debt within 3–4 years? Is equity growing through retained earnings?
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### STEP 3 — LIQUIDITY
>> ### *Liquidity* Check
>> End with the Cash Flow. Is operating cash flow positive? Does cash from operations match net income? Heavy divergence signals earnings quality issues.

> [!example] Fraud Detection Tip
> Compare the **tax paid** on the income statement against the stated **Profit Before Tax (PBT)**. If a company claims high profits but pays unusually low taxes, they may have overstated their profit — a common fraud signal.
