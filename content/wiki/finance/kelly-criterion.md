# Kelly Criterion

<p class="at-lead">
The Kelly Criterion is a formula for determining the optimal size of a series of bets or investments to maximise the logarithm of wealth over time. It balances the desire to exploit an edge against the risk of ruin from over-betting.
</p>

## Overview

For a simple bet with probability $p$ of winning and net odds $b$, the Kelly fraction is $f^* = \frac{bp - q}{b}$ where $q = 1-p$. In investing, it generalises to allocating capital across assets with expected returns and covariances. The criterion maximises expected geometric growth rate and mathematically prevents total loss.

In practice, many investors use **fractional Kelly** (e.g., half-Kelly) to reduce volatility, since full Kelly can experience severe drawdowns. The criterion has been applied by Ed Thorp in blackjack and investing, Warren Buffett in position sizing, and quant funds in portfolio construction.
