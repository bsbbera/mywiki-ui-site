# Monopoly

<p class="at-lead">
A Monopoly is a market structure in which a single firm is the sole seller of a product with no close substitutes, giving it substantial control over price and supply. It represents the extreme opposite of perfect competition and is a central topic in microeconomics and antitrust law.
</p>

## Overview

Monopolies arise from barriers to entry such as control of essential resources, government-granted exclusive rights (patents, licenses), network effects, economies of scale (natural monopoly), or predatory tactics. A profit-maximising monopolist sets output where marginal revenue equals marginal cost, resulting in higher prices and lower quantities than competitive markets — creating **deadweight loss**.

Governments regulate or break up monopolies through antitrust legislation (Sherman Act, Clayton Act in the US; competition law in the EU). Debates continue over whether some monopolies (e.g., utilities, platform tech companies) should be regulated, nationalised, or left to market forces.
