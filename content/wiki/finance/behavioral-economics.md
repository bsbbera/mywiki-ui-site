# Behavioral Economics

<p class="at-lead">
Behavioral economics integrates psychological insights into economic models, showing that human decisions systematically deviate from classical rationality. Concepts such as loss aversion, mental accounting, anchoring, and present bias explain why people violate expected utility theory and why markets are not always efficient.
</p>

## Overview

Pioneered by Kahneman, Tversky, Thaler, and others, behavioural economics replaced the homo economicus with a more realistic decision-maker. The Allais paradox and Ellsberg paradox provided early empirical evidence that preferences depend on framing, certainty, and ambiguity, not just outcomes and probabilities. Policy applications include nudge theory, default options, and behavioural public policy.
