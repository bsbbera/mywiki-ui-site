# Fiscal Policy

<p class="at-lead">
Fiscal Policy refers to government decisions on spending, taxation, and borrowing to influence economic activity, employment, inflation, and growth. Alongside monetary policy, it is one of the two primary levers for macroeconomic management.
</p>

## Overview

**Expansionary fiscal policy** increases government spending or cuts taxes to stimulate demand during recessions. **Contractionary fiscal policy** reduces spending or raises taxes to cool down an overheating economy and curb inflation. The effectiveness depends on multipliers, crowding-out effects, debt sustainability, and the state of the business cycle.

Key debates include Keynesian vs classical views, the Ricardian equivalence proposition, the optimal size of government, and the role of automatic stabilisers (unemployment benefits, progressive taxes) vs discretionary stimulus. High public debt-to-GDP ratios constrain fiscal space and raise intergenerational equity concerns.
