# Nash Equilibrium

<p class="at-lead">
In a Nash equilibrium, every player's strategy is optimal given the strategies of all other players. No unilateral deviation can improve a player's payoff. Named after John Nash, who proved that every finite game has at least one equilibrium in mixed strategies, it is the central solution concept in non-cooperative game theory.
</p>

## Overview

Nash equilibrium applies to oligopoly pricing, auction design, evolutionary biology, and network routing. Yet it can produce counterintuitive outcomes: in the Bertrand paradox, two firms in Nash equilibrium earn zero profits. Refinements such as subgame perfection and trembling-hand perfection eliminate weak or implausible equilibria in dynamic and uncertain settings.
