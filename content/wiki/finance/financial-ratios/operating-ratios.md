# Operating Ratios

<p class="at-lead">
Operating ratios — also called Activity Ratios or Management Ratios — measure the efficiency with which a business converts its assets into revenue. They are the direct lens into how well management is running the business day-to-day: how fast inventory moves, how quickly cash is collected, and how productively assets are deployed.
</p>

<span class="at-stat">6 key ratios</span> &nbsp;·&nbsp; <span class="at-stat">Inventory · DSO · Asset Turnover · Working Capital</span> &nbsp;·&nbsp; <span class="at-mark">the management efficiency scorecard</span>

<span class="at-kicker">Inventory Ratios</span>

## Inventory Management Ratios

### Inventory Turnover Ratio

$$\text{Inventory Turnover} = \frac{\text{Cost of Goods Sold}}{\text{Average Inventory}}$$

Inventory turnover measures **how quickly a company sells through its inventory** in a given period. A product with high demand will have a high turnover ratio — it leaves the warehouse faster.

> [!info] Why COGS (not Revenue)?
> Using COGS (not revenue) avoids the distortion from profit margins. COGS represents the actual cost of inventory, making the ratio a true measure of physical inventory movement.

**Higher inventory turnover = better demand for products = stronger business.**

### Inventory Number of Days

$$\text{Inventory Days} = \frac{365}{\text{Inventory Turnover}}$$

Inventory Days (also called Days Inventory Outstanding) gives a sense of **how many days it takes the company to convert its inventory to cash**.

> [!tip] The Simpler Rule
> **Lesser the number of days, the better.** A company that sells through its inventory in 30 days is far more efficient than one taking 120 days. Monitor the trend over time — rising inventory days can signal slowing demand.

> [!grid|cols2]
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### HIGH TURNOVER (GOOD)
>> ### *High* Inventory Turnover
>> Products sell quickly, working capital is freed up, less storage cost, less risk of obsolescence. Typical of consumer staples, fast fashion, grocery retail.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### LOW TURNOVER (WATCH)
>> ### *Low* Inventory Turnover
>> Products sit in warehouses. Either demand is weak or the company is over-producing. For manufacturing companies, rising inventory without rising PAT margin is a serious red flag.

<span class="at-kicker">Receivables Ratios</span>

## Receivables Management Ratios

### Accounts Receivable Turnover Ratio

$$\text{AR Turnover} = \frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average Receivables}}$$

The AR Turnover ratio indicates **how many times in a given period the company collects cash from its debtors and customers**.

**Higher AR Turnover = more frequent cash collection = healthier cash cycle.**

> [!warning] Sales Backed by Receivables
> If revenue is growing but receivables are growing faster than sales, the company may be **pushing products to record revenue** without customers actually paying. This signals potential earnings quality issues — see [[../financial-statements/balance-sheet|Balance Sheet]] receivables discussion.

### Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)

$$\text{DSO} = \frac{365}{\text{AR Turnover Ratio}}$$

DSO measures the **average number of days it takes for a company to collect payment** after a sale. Lower DSO = better cash conversion.

| DSO Range | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| **< 30 days** | Excellent — near-immediate cash collection |
| **30 – 60 days** | Normal for B2B businesses |
| **60 – 90 days** | Watch — extended credit terms |
| **> 90 days** | Concern — potential bad debt buildup |

<span class="at-kicker">Accounts Receivable & Payable</span>

## The AR and AP Relationship

**Accounts Receivable (Net Receivables)** is the balance of money due to the firm for goods/services delivered but not yet paid. Listed on the Balance Sheet as a Current Asset.

> Net Receivables = Gross Receivables − Estimated Bad Debt Allowance

**Accounts Payable** is similar — but money the company *owes* to its suppliers. It is a Current Liability.

> [!tip] Competitive Advantage Signal
> A company with **consistently lower Net Receivables / Gross Sales ratio** compared to its competitors has a competitive advantage — it collects cash more efficiently and has better quality customers.

<span class="at-kicker">Asset Utilisation Ratios</span>

## Asset Utilisation Ratios

### Total Asset Turnover

$$\text{Total Asset Turnover} = \frac{\text{Operating Revenue}}{\text{Average Total Assets}}$$

Total Asset Turnover measures the **company's capability to generate revenues with its total assets** — both fixed assets and current assets.

`Higher Total Asset Turnover compared to historical data and competitor data means the company is using its assets well to generate more sales.`

### Working Capital Turnover Ratio

**Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities**

A company with:
- **Positive Working Capital** → working capital surplus
- **Negative Working Capital** → working capital deficit

$$\text{Working Capital Turnover} = \frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average Working Capital}}$$

**Higher Working Capital Turnover = the company generates better sales relative to the money it uses to fund those sales.**

> [!info] Working Capital Context
> Working capital represents the **capital required to run day-to-day operations**. A well-managed company finances current assets with current liabilities — the difference is the net working capital requirement. The Working Capital Turnover Ratio measures how efficiently this net capital generates sales.

## All Operating Ratios at a Glance

>> ###### INVENTORY TURNOVER
>> ### *Inventory* Turnover
>> `COGS / Avg Inventory`
>> Higher = demand-driven business. Sector-dependent: grocery high (>15×), industrial low (3–5×).
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### INVENTORY DAYS
>> ### *Inventory* Days
>> `365 / Inventory Turnover`
>> Days to sell inventory. Lower is better. Rising days = slowing demand signal.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### AR TURNOVER
>> ### *AR* Turnover
>> `Revenue / Avg Receivables`
>> Higher = faster cash collection. Watch divergence from revenue growth.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### DSO
>> ### *Days Sales* Outstanding
>> `365 / AR Turnover`
>> Days to collect cash from customers. Lower is better. Compare to industry norms.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### ASSET TURNOVER
>> ### *Total Asset* Turnover
>> `Operating Revenue / Avg Total Assets`
>> Revenue generated per unit of total assets. Compare to historical trend and peers.
>
>> [!card|section]
>> ###### WORKING CAPITAL TURNOVER
>> ### *Working Capital* Turnover
>> `Revenue / Avg Working Capital`
>> How much revenue is generated per unit of net working capital deployed.

<span class="at-kicker">Trend Analysis</span>

## Trend Analysis — The Key to Operating Ratios

Operating ratios are most valuable when **tracked over multiple years** and compared to peers:

| Signal | What to Check | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rising Inventory Days** | Compare YoY | Demand slowdown or inventory build-up |
| **Rising DSO** | Compare YoY | Customers paying slower; receivables quality concern |
| **Falling Asset Turnover** | Compare YoY and peers | Assets becoming less productive |
| **Rising WC Turnover** | Compare YoY | Improving efficiency, better capital utilisation |
| **Receivables > Revenue growth** | Side-by-side comparison | Aggressive revenue recognition warning |

> [!example] Manufacturing Company Watch
> For manufacturing companies, always cross-check:
> - Growing inventory + growing PAT margin → **Positive** — profitable demand expansion
> - Growing inventory + flat/declining PAT margin → **Negative** — boom-bust cycle, potential oversupply
