# Oligopoly

<p class="at-lead">
An oligopoly is a market structure dominated by a small number of firms whose decisions are interdependent. Pricing, output, and advertising choices by one firm directly affect the profits of others, creating strategic complexity modelled by game theory. Examples include airlines, telecommunications, and automobile manufacturing.
</p>

## Overview

Key oligopoly models include Cournot competition (firms choose quantities), Bertrand competition (firms choose prices), and collusion or cartel behaviour. The Bertrand paradox shows that with homogeneous products and no capacity constraints, duopoly behaves like perfect competition, driving prices to marginal cost and eliminating profits.
