# Prospect Theory

<p class="at-lead">
Prospect theory describes how people actually make decisions under risk. Rather than maximising expected utility, decision-makers evaluate outcomes relative to a reference point, overweight small probabilities, underweight large probabilities, and feel losses roughly twice as intensely as equivalent gains. It replaced expected utility as the dominant descriptive model of risky choice.
</p>

## Overview

Kahneman and Tversky formulated prospect theory in 1979, building directly on the Allais paradox and Ellsberg paradox. The theory introduced the value function (S-shaped, steeper for losses than gains) and probability weighting (inverse-S shaped). It explains insurance, gambling, the equity premium puzzle, and many market anomalies that rational models cannot.
