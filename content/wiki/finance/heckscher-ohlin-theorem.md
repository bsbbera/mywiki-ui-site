# Heckscher–Ohlin Theorem

<p class="at-lead">
The Heckscher–Ohlin theorem states that countries export goods that intensively use their relatively abundant factors of production and import goods that intensively use their scarce factors. A capital-abundant country exports capital-intensive goods; a labour-abundant country exports labour-intensive goods. The Leontief paradox famously challenged the theorem with empirical evidence from the United States.
</p>

## Overview

The H–O model extends Ricardo's comparative advantage theory by explaining the pattern of trade through factor endowments rather than technology differences. Its predictions about the distributional effects of trade — that opening to trade benefits a country's abundant factor and harms its scarce factor — remain central to debates on globalisation, wages, and inequality.
