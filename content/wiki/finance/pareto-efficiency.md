# Pareto Efficiency

<p class="at-lead">
Pareto Efficiency is a state of resource allocation in which it is impossible to reallocate resources to make any one individual better off without making at least one individual worse off. It is a minimal criterion for economic efficiency, widely used in welfare economics, mechanism design, and contract theory.
</p>

## Overview

Pareto efficiency does not imply fairness or equity — a dictatorship where one person owns everything can be Pareto optimal. The First Fundamental Theorem of Welfare Economics states that competitive equilibria are Pareto efficient under certain assumptions (perfect competition, no externalities, complete markets).

When markets fail, policies like taxation, regulation, or public goods provision may improve welfare in a **Kaldor-Hicks** sense (total gains exceed total losses, allowing hypothetical compensation) even if not a pure Pareto improvement. Pareto analysis also refers to the 80/20 rule in business and quality management.
