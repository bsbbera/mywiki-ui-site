# Cosmology

<p class="at-lead">
Cosmology is the scientific study of the origin, evolution, and eventual fate of the universe. It combines observations of the cosmic microwave background, galaxy distributions, and supernovae with general relativity and particle physics to construct models of cosmic history.
</p>

## Overview

The standard model of cosmology — Lambda-CDM — describes a universe born in the Big Bang approximately 13.8 billion years ago, composed of ordinary matter (~5%), dark matter (~27%), and dark energy (~68%). Key epochs include inflation, nucleosynthesis, recombination, and the formation of the first stars and galaxies.

Cosmologists probe questions about the flatness of space, the arrow of time, the multiverse hypothesis, and the ultimate fate of the universe (big freeze, big crunch, or big rip). Precision measurements from missions like Planck and JWST continue to refine our cosmic parameters.
