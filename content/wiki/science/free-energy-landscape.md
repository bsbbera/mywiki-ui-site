# Free-Energy Landscape

<p class="at-lead">
The free-energy landscape is a conceptual map plotting the potential energy of a system against its configuration coordinates. In protein folding, the landscape is funneled — biased toward the native state by hydrophobic collapse and cooperative interactions — resolving the Levinthal paradox by showing that folding is guided, not random.
</p>

## Overview

Rather than exploring all possible conformations, proteins slide downhill on a rugged but globally funneled landscape. Local minima represent misfolded intermediates, while the global minimum is the native functional structure. The landscape paradigm unifies kinetics and thermodynamics, explaining both rapid folding and the existence of folding diseases.
