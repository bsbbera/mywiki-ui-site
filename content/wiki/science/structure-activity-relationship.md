# Structure–Activity Relationship

<p class="at-lead">
Structure–activity relationship (SAR) is the principle that a molecule's biological or chemical activity can be predicted from its structural features. While generally reliable, exceptions are frequent and profound — a single atom swap can abolish activity or convert an agonist into an antagonist, driving the SAR paradox in drug design.
</p>

## Overview

SAR underpins medicinal chemistry, toxicology, and materials science. Chemists systematically vary functional groups, stereocentres, and scaffolds to map how structure modulates binding, potency, and selectivity. The existence of "activity cliffs" — structurally similar molecules with wildly different potencies — reminds us that biological systems amplify tiny structural differences into large functional outcomes.
