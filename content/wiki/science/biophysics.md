# Biophysics

<p class="at-lead">
Biophysics is an interdisciplinary science that applies theories and methods from physics to understand biological phenomena. It spans molecular structures, cellular mechanics, neurophysics, and ecosystem dynamics, bridging the gap between physics and biology.
</p>

## Overview

Biophysicists study how molecules fold and interact (structural biophysics), how cells generate and use forces (mechanobiology), how neurons process information (neurophysics), and how organisms sense electromagnetic fields. Techniques include cryo-electron microscopy, single-molecule spectroscopy, and patch-clamp electrophysiology.

The field has produced landmark discoveries: the double helix structure of DNA, the mechanism of photosynthesis, ion channel gating, and protein folding pathways. Today, computational biophysics uses molecular dynamics simulations to model biological processes at atomic resolution.
