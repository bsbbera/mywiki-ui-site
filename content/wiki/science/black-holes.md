# Black Holes

<p class="at-lead">
Black holes are regions of spacetime where gravity is so intense that nothing — not even light — can escape once it crosses the event horizon. They are predicted by Einstein's general relativity and supported by overwhelming observational evidence.
</p>

## Overview

Stellar-mass black holes form when massive stars exhaust nuclear fuel and collapse under their own gravity. Supermassive black holes (millions to billions of solar masses) reside at the centres of most galaxies, including our own Milky Way. Intermediate-mass and primordial black holes remain hypothetical or rare.

Key concepts include the **event horizon** (the point of no return), the **singularity** (where density and spacetime curvature diverge), **Hawking radiation** (quantum emission from the horizon), and **spaghettification** (tidal stretching near the horizon). The 2019 Event Horizon Telescope image of M87* provided the first direct visual evidence.
