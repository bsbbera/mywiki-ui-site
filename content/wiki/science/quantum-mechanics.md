# Quantum Mechanics

<p class="at-lead">
Quantum mechanics is the fundamental theory describing nature at the smallest scales — atoms, photons, and subatomic particles. It replaces classical determinism with wavefunctions, superposition, entanglement, and probabilistic measurement outcomes, giving rise to paradoxes such as Schrödinger's cat and the EPR paradox.
</p>

## Overview

The Schrödinger equation governs how quantum states evolve over time, while measurement collapses the wavefunction into definite eigenstates. Quantum mechanics is the most precisely tested theory in physics, yet its interpretation — Copenhagen, many-worlds, pilot-wave, or decoherence — remains philosophically contested. It underpins chemistry, materials science, and all modern electronics.
