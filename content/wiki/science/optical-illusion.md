# Optical Illusion

<p class="at-lead">
Optical illusions are visual stimuli that cause the brain to perceive something different from physical reality. They reveal the heuristics the visual system uses to compress and interpret the world — lateral inhibition, predictive coding, depth cues, and colour constancy — all of which can be strategically fooled by clever stimulus design.
</p>

## Overview

From the Hermann grid and Mach bands to impossible figures and the Ames room, optical illusions expose the inferential nature of perception. The brain does not passively photograph the world; it actively constructs a workable model based on priors, assumptions, and evolutionary biases. Illusions are not failures — they are the price of a system optimised for speed and robustness.
