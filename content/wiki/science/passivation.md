# Passivation

<p class="at-lead">
Passivation is the spontaneous formation of a protective oxide film on a metal surface that prevents further corrosion. Concentrated nitric acid passivates steel, while dilute acid attacks it — a counterintuitive result that launched the study of electrode kinetics and surface electrochemistry.
</p>

## Overview

Passivation explains why stainless steel resists rust, why aluminium protects itself, and why concentrated oxidising acids can paradoxically reduce corrosion. The passive film is typically only a few nanometres thick, yet it dramatically lowers the corrosion current by blocking electron transfer and ion diffusion at the metal–solution interface.
