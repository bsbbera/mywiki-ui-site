# Astrophysics

<p class="at-lead">
Astrophysics is the branch of astronomy that employs the principles of physics and chemistry to understand the nature of celestial bodies and the physical processes that govern the universe — from stars and galaxies to dark matter and cosmic evolution.
</p>

## Overview

Astrophysicists investigate stellar structure and evolution, galaxy formation, cosmology, high-energy phenomena (black holes, neutron stars, gamma-ray bursts), and the large-scale structure of the universe. Observational astrophysics relies on telescopes across the electromagnetic spectrum, gravitational wave detectors, and neutrino observatories.

Theoretical astrophysics uses general relativity, quantum mechanics, plasma physics, and thermodynamics to model cosmic phenomena. Major open questions include the nature of dark matter and dark energy, the physics of black hole singularities, and the origin of cosmic magnetic fields.
