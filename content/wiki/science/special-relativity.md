# Special Relativity

<p class="at-lead">
Einstein's special theory of relativity (1905) postulates that the laws of physics are identical in all inertial frames and that the speed of light in a vacuum is constant for all observers. From these two principles follow time dilation, length contraction, mass–energy equivalence (E = mc²), and the relativity of simultaneity.
</p>

## Overview

Special relativity revolutionised classical mechanics by showing that space and time are not absolute but interwoven into a four-dimensional spacetime. The Lorentz transformations replace Galilean relativity, ensuring that Maxwell's equations hold in all inertial frames. The theory is essential for particle physics, astrophysics, and GPS satellite corrections.
