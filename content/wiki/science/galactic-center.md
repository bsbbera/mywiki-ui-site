# Galactic Center

<p class="at-lead">
The Galactic Center is the rotational center of the Milky Way, located roughly 26,000 light-years from Earth in the direction of the constellation Sagittarius. It harbours Sagittarius A*, a supermassive black hole surrounded by a dense cluster of stars, molecular clouds, and magnetic structures that continue to challenge models of star formation and accretion physics.
</p>

## Overview

Observations in radio, infrared, and X-ray bands reveal a complex environment where tidal forces should disrupt star formation, yet young stars exist surprisingly close to the black hole. The paradox of youth — an overabundance of young stars near Sgr A* — may be explained by infall of stellar clusters or episodic star formation in a dense accretion disk.
