# Brønsted Catalysis Law

<p class="at-lead">
The Brønsted catalysis law states that the catalytic efficacy of an acid or base is linearly related to its dissociation constant. Discovered through the Brønsted paradox — in which buffer concentration accelerated reactions at constant pH — the law transformed an anomaly into the foundational principle of general-acid and general-base catalysis.
</p>

## Overview

Brønsted showed that any proton donor or acceptor can catalyse a reaction, not just H₃O⁺ or OH⁻. The rate constant for catalysis is proportional to the acid or base dissociation constant raised to a fractional power (the Brønsted α or β). This principle underpins modern enzyme mechanism studies, where multiple acidic residues cooperate in the active site.
